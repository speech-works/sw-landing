# Voice of customer: how adults who stammer talk about it

Step 4 of the copy validation plan. Desk research only, carried out 18 September 2026.

**What was searched:**
- TISA (stammer.in): Indian first-person posts, the main source
- The Better India
- STAMMA (UK)
- the Stuttering Foundation blog
- App Store reviews of Stamurai

**Limits:**
- Reddit, Quora and the Google Play pages blocked automated access.
- No search-volume data was available.
- Quotes were pulled out by a fetch tool, so check each one against its source before using it in public copy.

## Headline findings

| # | Finding | Evidence | What it means for copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Indians say "stammer", not "stutter".** | The national group is The Indian Stammering Association. Stamurai names its Indian listing "Stammering Treatment" and its US one "Stuttering Treatment". Indian clinics lead with "stammering". The Hindi word is *haklana*. | The site says "stutter" everywhere and "stammering" once, in the FAQ. Lead with "stammer", and add "(stutter)" once. |
| 2 | **Job interviews and campus placements are the #1 Indian pain.** | "I was rejected in 6 interviews…"; "I am NOT getting placement due to stammering"; "i couldn't sleep yesterday night because of the fear". | The interview program is the right lead. Use the words "placement" and "interview". |
| 3 | **Fear before speaking comes up more than the stammer itself.** | "I was rehearsing to fail, creating an internal movie of my own failure."; "my heart rate would increase as my turn came to speak". | Rehearsing out loud is the answer to rehearsing failure in your head. This is the strongest angle for the headline. |
| 4 | **Phone calls are the second concrete pain.** | "Every phone call was a test."; "too scared to phone my friend in case I might stammer". | AI phone call practice fits this directly. |
| 5 | **People avoid and swap words.** | Changed "Butter" to another word; "Some of those, please"; skipped the fresher's welcome. | Matches the Word Swap and Panic Button programs. |
| 6 | **People start by wanting a cure and end up wanting confidence.** | Early posts: "haklan theek kar do", "considerable fluency before a job". Later: "I can then easily say whatever u want to say", "Stammer as much as you want, just please don't stop speaking." | Meet them at the situation ("interview", "phone call"), then deliver on confidence and on saying what they want. Never promise fluency. |
| 7 | **Clinic therapy doesn't carry over into real life.** | "I didn't stammer on a single word… After you know what I came back to same." (NIMHANS); cure centres whose techniques were "known to me already". | Position Speechworks as practice for the real situation, not another technique course. |
| 8 | **Subscriptions and billing are the top complaint about apps.** | Stamurai reviews: "20 a week? No way.", "my money is gone", paid but not activated, exercises not matched to the user. | "Pay once" is a real differentiator. Say it early. |

## Their words vs. our words

| They say | Site says now | Use |
|---|---|---|
| stammer, stammering, haklana | stutter | "stammer (stutter)" |
| placement, interview, HR round | job interview | "placement or job interview" |
| every phone call is a test | a phone call | "the calls you put off" |
| fear, heart racing, can't sleep the night before | "feel worried about speaking" | "the night-before fear" (soft wording, no promise) |
| change the word, avoid | "words you often replace" | "words you swap or avoid" |
| say whatever I want to say | "Say what you want to say" (version B) | keep. It's almost exactly their words. |
| confidence, courage, don't stop speaking | (not used) | "keep talking", "at your pace" |

## Words to avoid

These come from community pushback:
- cure, fix
- "just relax", "slow down", "take a deep breath"
- suffer, sufferer
- anything that defines someone by their speech

"Stutterer" and "stammerer" split opinion. Some Indian writers use them about themselves, but the copy should not.

## Top themes, ranked by how often they came up
1. Job interviews and placements
2. Fear and rehearsing failure before speaking
3. Phone calls
4. Shame, avoidance and word switching
5. Disclosure and acceptance as the turning point
